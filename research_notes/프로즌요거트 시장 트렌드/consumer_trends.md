# Consumer Trends Shaping Frozen Yogurt / Frozen Dessert Category (US focus)

## How is the "health halo" / better-for-you positioning of frozen yogurt evolving (protein, low sugar, probiotic/gut health, functional ingredients)?

### Takeaway
Frozen yogurt's health positioning has shifted from generic "low-fat" messaging (2000s-2010s) to a sharper focus on high protein and low/no added sugar, with probiotic/gut-health claims layered on top — though independent commentary warns the gut-health claims are often more marketing than substance, and operators report real formulation friction getting high-protein whey through pasteurization. Simultaneously, several operators are explicitly de-emphasizing "diet food" framing in favor of "indulgence" and lifestyle positioning, creating tension with the health-halo narrative.

### Cited Findings
- The global frozen yogurt market was valued at ~$1.87-1.93 billion in 2025, projected to reach $2.55-2.65 billion by 2034, with "expanded purchaser inclination for non-dairy, low-calorie, high-protein, and more [healthful] desserts" cited as the primary driver — [MarketDataForecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/frozen-yogurt-market); [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)
- Approximately 44% of new frozen yogurt product launches include probiotic claims, and 37% feature reduced-sugar formulations; about 50% of young adults (18-30) say they choose frozen yogurt specifically for its lower fat content and higher protein value — [MarketsandMarkets blog](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/blog/FB/Protein-Yogurt)
- High protein is described as "the defining characteristic and a top health claim globally" for yogurt, with low/no-added-sugar framed as especially important in North America — [Glanbia Nutrition](https://www.glanbianutrition.com/en/nutri-knowledge-center/insights/yogurt-trends-watch); [Innova Market Insights](https://www.innovamarketinsights.com/trends/global-yogurt-trends-2025/)
- 16 Handles CEO Neil Hershman explicitly frames the resurgence as a reaction against indulgent dessert trends: "People were waiting in line for high-calorie cookies, but some want a healthier dessert they can have more often" — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Hershman also notes a real operational constraint on the protein trend: "it's almost impossible to run really high-protein whey powder through pasteurization" — meaning high-protein froyo claims face genuine manufacturing limits, not just marketing choices — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- 16 Handles has run limited-edition kefir probiotic soft serve as a health-forward flavor extension — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Go Greek Yogurt markets itself around "sparking a healthy living revolution," built on a self-serve bar featuring mainly sugar-free toppings — [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)
- Counter-narrative/skepticism: consumer-health commentary notes frozen yogurt products are not held to the same regulatory standard as refrigerated dairy yogurt, "many contain no live cultures at all," most are "loaded with sugar," and frozen yogurt "often delivers far fewer probiotics than traditional, refrigerated yogurt" — it's "some is better than none" rather than a real gut-health powerhouse — [Cymbiotika](https://cymbiotika.com/blogs/health-hub/does-frozen-yogurt-still-have-probiotics-exploring-the-truth-behind-this-popular-treat); [Zenwise](https://zenwise.com/blogs/healthy-gut/is-frozen-yogurt-good-for-gut-health-the-sweet-truth)
- Brand/ingredient-level activations tying froyo explicitly to gut health: Coconut Cult (a cultured/probiotic coconut product) partnered with froyo retailers Nude Miami, Happier Grocery, and Sunlife Organics for limited-edition offerings; fiber brand Benefiber ran a "Good Gut Club" pop-up at Washington Square Park (NYC) — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Industry trend piece frames a notable repositioning away from health framing: "what once read as a 'better-for-you' dessert now feels like an everyday treat," with shops "swapping diet culture for indulgence" — [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/usa/news/the-frozen-yogurt-revival-is-officialand-the-fro-yo-is-better-than-you-remember-020426) (note: this is a lifestyle/trend-journalism piece, more opinion than data-driven)
- Mintel's "Future of Ice Cream 2025" report (paid report; only publicly summarized headline available) lists "fro-yo will gain new purpose" as a named 2025 trend, alongside a separate Mintel finding that, for US ice cream broadly, "indulgence tops health" — suggesting the category-wide pull toward indulgence messaging even as froyo specifically carves out a health-adjacent niche — [Mintel Store listing](https://store.mintel.com/report/the-future-of-ice-cream); [Mintel press release, "Indulgence tops health for US ice cream"](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/indulgence-tops-health-for-us-ice-cream/)
- Danone launched fiber-fortified and immunity-supporting probiotic yogurt lines in 2025 aimed at health-conscious consumers (dairy yogurt, not frozen specifically, but indicates where functional-ingredient innovation is concentrated in the broader yogurt category) — [Future Market Insights](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/yogurt-and-probiotic-drink-market)

### Inferences
- The "health halo" is becoming bifurcated: operationally/scientifically credible claims (high protein as a satiety/fitness lever) are being pursued seriously, while gut-health/probiotic claims function more as marketing signaling than verified functional benefit — a gap a marketing team should be careful not to overstate to avoid credibility risk.
- There is a real strategic tension in the category between "health-forward" messaging (protein, low sugar, probiotics) and the newer "indulgence/lifestyle" repositioning being pursued by viral NYC shops — a chain's marketing choice here is a real differentiation lever, not a settled consensus.

### Gaps
- No independent, verified sales-lift data (e.g., same-store sales attributable specifically to a protein or probiotic SKU launch) was found for any named froyo chain — the 16 Handles and Go Greek figures found are about overall same-store sales/revenue growth, not isolated to health-forward product lines.
- Could not locate full Mintel or Technomic report content (paywalled); only headline/press-release-level summaries were accessible.

## What is the state of plant-based and dairy-free demand within frozen yogurt and soft serve specifically?

### Takeaway
Dairy-free/plant-based frozen soft serve is a genuine, fast-growing sub-segment (double-digit CAGR forecasts) driven by lactose intolerance, allergen avoidance, and broader plant-based dietary interest, with North America currently the largest market by volume and Asia-Pacific the fastest-growing; however, most of the hard data found is market-research-firm forecasting (speculative/model-based) rather than verified point-of-sale figures from specific froyo chains.

### Cited Findings
- The global "frozen dairy-free soft serve mix" market was valued at $1.1 billion in 2024 and is forecast to reach $2.7 billion by 2033 (a 10.3% CAGR) — [MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/frozen-dairy-free-soft-serve-mix-market) (note: forward-looking market-sizing forecast from a market research vendor; treat as a directional estimate, not verified actuals)
- Within the broader frozen yogurt market, "non-dairy and plant-based frozen yogurt" is forecast to grow at an 11.85% CAGR through 2035 — described as "the fastest [growth rate] of any product cut" in that forecast — [search-aggregated frozen yogurt market report] (same caveat: vendor forecast, not verified)
- North America accounted for roughly 38% of global dairy-free soft-serve-mix sales in 2024, attributed to a developed foodservice industry and high existing adoption of plant-based products; Asia-Pacific is projected to have the highest CAGR (13.1%) going forward, driven by urbanization, rising incomes, and higher rates of lactose intolerance in the region — [MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/frozen-dairy-free-soft-serve-mix-market)
- At the product level, vegan soft serve made with alternative milks (cashew, almond, coconut, oat) was the most common dairy-free froyo flavor type tracked, representing 31% of new dairy-free froyo flavor launches in a 2019 industry snapshot — [International Frozen Yogurt Association, "2019 The Year in Froyo Review"](https://internationalfrozenyogurt.com/2019-the-year-in-froyo-review/) (dated — labeled as historical context, not a current 2024-2026 figure)
- At the chain level: TCBY offers a dedicated dairy-free/vegan menu — [GoDairyFree.org, "TCBY Dairy-Free & Vegan Menu Guide"](https://godairyfree.org/news/tcby-dairy-free-frozen-yogurt"); 16 Handles offers vegan options including oat-milk soft serve and fruit sorbets as part of its expanded lineup — [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)
- Mintel's "Future of Ice Cream 2025" headline notes that, for ice cream broadly (not froyo specifically), "plant-based ice creams are failing to gain further ground" — a signal that plant-based adoption dynamics may differ between ice cream and frozen yogurt/soft serve, and that plant-based claims in the wider frozen-dessert category are not uniformly a growth story — [Mintel Store listing](https://store.mintel.com/report/the-future-of-ice-cream)

### Inferences
- The strongest, most specific plant-based signal is in soft-serve mix/base supply (B2B ingredient market) rather than in consumer-facing froyo chain same-store sales — suggesting dairy-free froyo is currently more of an infrastructure/menu-expansion trend (chains adding a vegan option to broaden audience) than a demand-led segment driving traffic on its own.
- The explicit Mintel finding that plant-based ice cream is stalling is a useful caution against assuming "plant-based" is an unambiguous growth lever across all frozen desserts; it may not transfer directly to froyo, and a marketing team should validate category-specific (not just ice-cream-wide) plant-based demand before over-indexing on it.

### Gaps
- No verified, named-chain sales or transaction data (e.g., "X% of orders now include a dairy-free substitution") could be found for any major US froyo chain (Pinkberry, Yogurtland, 16 Handles, TCBY, Menchie's).
- No Mintel/Technomic/Datassential dairy-free-specific froyo adoption-rate figure was accessible (reports are paywalled; only headlines/press summaries were available).
- Could not confirm whether the 11.85% CAGR figure for "non-dairy and plant-based frozen yogurt" comes from a named, citable primary report — it surfaced only in aggregated search-result summarization and the original source/report name could not be verified. Flag this figure as lower-confidence.

## Is there a "premiumization" trend in frozen yogurt (higher-end ingredients, artisanal positioning, elevated toppings), and who is leading it?

### Takeaway
Premiumization is one of the best-documented and most consistent trends in the current froyo cycle: elevated, often savory-leaning toppings (pistachio cream, cold-pressed olive oil, Maldon sea salt, wildflower honey, matcha, mochi) and price points up to ~$20/serving are displacing the old "pay-by-weight self-serve bar" model, led by a new wave of independent/boutique NYC and Nashville shops (Birdie's, Mimi's, Myka, Culture Club) alongside established chains like Pinkberry repositioning around premium brand equity.

### Cited Findings
- Pinkberry is identified as the leading premium frozen yogurt brand, with a cited "9.1/10 Premium Brand Equity score" allowing it to sustain a 15-20% price premium over competitors, built on its tangy yogurt base and premium toppings — [VerifiedMarketResearch](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/blog/top-frozen-yogurt-companies/) (note: this specific brand-equity score comes from a market-research vendor blog; treat the precise figure as vendor-sourced rather than independently audited)
- American consumers are reportedly willing to spend as much as $20 on a protein-packed frozen yogurt topped with premium ingredients ranging "from pistachio cream to olive oil" — [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)
- At Birdie's and Culture Club, customers top creamy swirled bases with Japanese mochi bites, pistachio cream, and drizzles of cold-pressed extra-virgin olive oil; the combination of olive oil and Maldon sea salt on sweet yogurt is described as having become "an absolute viral sensation" — [search-aggregated trend summary, corroborated across Fox News/AOL and Modern Retail reporting]
- Culture Club (Nashville) offers an elevated topping range spanning fruits, jams, nuts, sauces, artisan honey, Maldon sea salt, and rose petals — [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)
- Mimi's (NYC) differentiates on ingredient sourcing: Greek yogurt base with milk, kefir, and cane sugar, plus matcha, real vanilla bean, and locally sourced seasonal fruit — [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)
- 16 Handles CEO Neil Hershman frames the shift explicitly as a move toward "a demand curve now for premium product and better experience where families or friends can hang out," contrasted with the prior generation's rapid, less disciplined unit growth — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- 16 Handles average annual per-store sales rose from $660,000 in 2022 (year of its acquisition by Hershman) to $805,000 in 2024, alongside a repositioning toward "premium branding" — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again); 16 Handles VP of Franchise Development Fred Frey attributes current growth to "category-leading unit economics, a surge in popularity of our product, and our premium branding" — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/surge-in-froyo-demand-drives-16-handles-coast-to-coast-expansion-and-strong-2025-sales-momentum-302655913.html)
- A structural shift away from the old self-serve/toppings-bar format toward smaller portions and counter service is tied directly to premiumization: "You're seeing more gourmet, sundae-ish creations go viral" rather than the 20-flavor self-serve wall — [search-aggregated trend summary citing SF Standard/Axios/Salon reporting on the Gen Z froyo revival]

### Inferences
- Premiumization and the format shift away from self-serve appear to be the same underlying trend: curated, counter-served, elevated-topping "sundae" experiences are both the premiumization vehicle and the mechanism that differentiates the new wave from the 2000s/2010s self-serve boom.
- Independent/boutique shops (Birdie's, Mimi's, Myka, Culture Club) currently appear to be setting the premium creative/trend agenda (viral topping combinations), while larger chains (16 Handles, Pinkberry) are following with premium rebranding and pricing power rather than originating the trend — useful competitive-positioning context for a chain deciding whether to lead or follow on premium toppings.

### Gaps
- Could not directly access the original QSR Magazine ("Is Froyo Ready for its Second Act?") or Axios nostalgia-comeback articles (both returned HTTP 403); findings on these topics are corroborated instead through Modern Retail, ICSC, Placer.ai, and PR Newswire, which independently covered similar ground, but the QSR/Axios framing and any unique data points in those specific pieces could not be verified directly.
- No verified data on topping-attach-rate or average ticket size specifically before vs. after a chain's premiumization push (e.g., average order value change) was found.

## Is there a nostalgia-driven or Gen Z-driven revival of self-serve froyo? What evidence exists?

### Takeaway
There is strong, multi-source, recent (mid-2025 through 2026) evidence of a real froyo revival driven by Gen Z, but the evidence points to a revival of the *frozen yogurt category* rather than of the *self-serve format* specifically — the format driving virality is explicitly counter-service with curated toppings, a deliberate departure from the 2000s-2010s self-serve/toppings-bar model, even as it trades on nostalgia for that earlier froyo era.

### Cited Findings
- "A decade after the self-serve boom of the late 2000s and early 2010s, froyo is once again drawing lines, from New York City's West Village to San Francisco's Mission" — but the shops going viral "aren't the ones with 20 different flavors and a self-serve toppings bar"; instead "smaller portions" and "counter service" are trending, with "more gourmet, sundae-ish creations" going viral — [search-aggregated summary of SF Standard, Axios, and related reporting]
- Headline framing from trade/consumer press directly ties the revival to Gen Z nostalgia for a specific era: "To Gen Z, Obama-era nostalgia tastes like froyo" — [Salon](https://www.salon.com/2025/06/25/to-gen-z-obama-era-nostalgia-tastes-like-froyo/) (opinion/culture piece — treat framing as cultural commentary, not hard data)
- "San Francisco is falling for frozen yogurt all over again" — local trend reporting corroborating the revival outside NYC — [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/08/09/sf-frozen-yogurt-trend/)
- Axios frames the trend explicitly as "the next nostalgia comeback: Fro-yo" (December 2025) — [Axios](https://axios.com/2025/12/06/frozen-yogurt-stores-nostalgia-comeback) (full article could not be fetched directly — HTTP 403 — so only the headline/framing is confirmed, not Axios's specific data points)
- Gen Z's affinity for "viral experiential shops," combined with interest in wellness benefits (gut health, high protein), is cited as the dual driver of the revival — these shops have "successfully rebranded frozen yogurt from a nostalgic childhood memory into a trendy, modern lifestyle hub" — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Quantified foot-traffic evidence (Placer.ai, covering Jan-July 2026): frozen yogurt chains posted positive year-over-year foot-traffic growth in nearly every week of H1 2026, "at times topping 18%" YoY, while traditional ice cream shops saw negative weeks dipping "as low as -8.5% YoY" in the same window — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia)
- Separately, ICSC cites Placer.ai data (Jan 2024-Nov 2025) showing froyo shop traffic grew faster than the general dessert category in 15 of the 23 months measured, with positive month-over-month growth in all but 4 months — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- Concrete reopening/expansion evidence: 16 Handles signed 25 new franchise deals and opened 8 new stores in 2025 (NY, TX, SC, FL), with 67 total stores open or in development and multiple new locations hitting $1M+ revenue in their first 12 months — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/surge-in-froyo-demand-drives-16-handles-coast-to-coast-expansion-and-strong-2025-sales-momentum-302655913.html); [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- Yogurtland (founded 2005, closed ~60 stores during the pandemic, now ~200 US locations, 80% in California) opened 3 franchise locations in 2025 and announced 13 new store agreements in December 2025 across CA, NV, AZ, and — for the first time — Idaho, projecting 22 openings in 2026 and a pipeline through 2028 — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- TCBY — peak of 3,000 global locations, 84% of which had closed by 2013 — was acquired by Pearl Street Equity in 2023 and has since grown to 125 domestic outlets (end of 2024), including a reopening in Oswego, Illinois, and a 10-location international expansion agreement in Qatar (2024) — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- Go Greek Yogurt (founded 2012) opened new locations in Dubai, NYC, and London within the past year, is on track for 50+ global locations by Q1 2027, and its London opening reportedly drew hour-long lines; its director of strategy and growth reports customers "coming in multiple times a week" — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Viral, design-forward NYC shops specifically named as driving social-media momentum: Myka, Mimi's, and Birdie's — described as "design-forward, protein-friendly lifestyle" brands generating lines "forming around the block"; some customers reportedly get "froyo for breakfast" to avoid the crowds — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia); [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Miami is also reported as being "in its frozen yogurt era again," per local food press — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/food-drink/its-back-miami-is-in-its-frozen-yogurt-era-again-40585885/) (headline/framing only; full article not fetched)

### Inferences
- The revival is real and multi-city (NYC, SF, Nashville, Miami), multi-chain (independent boutiques + legacy chains), and traffic-data-backed (Placer.ai), which distinguishes it from a purely social-media-driven, unverified "trend piece" story — this is one of the best-evidenced findings in this research.
- However, the "self-serve" format itself is not what's reviving — the format innovation is explicitly a reaction against self-serve (smaller, curated, counter-served portions). A marketing team should not assume that reviving a self-serve toppings bar model would capture this demand; the evidence points toward curated/counter-service as the format consumers are responding to now.

### Gaps
- Could not directly access the Axios and QSR Magazine articles (403 errors), so cannot confirm whether they contain additional unique data points (e.g., specific unit-economics figures or named competitors) beyond what was corroborated via Modern Retail, Placer.ai, and ICSC.
- No data found on whether any of the original 2000s-2010s self-serve chains have attempted to pivot their format toward counter-service/curated in response to this trend (vs. simply opening new self-serve units) — this would be a useful follow-up question for strategic positioning.

## What consumer occasions/motivations are driving visits (treat-yourself, social/Instagrammable, kids/family, post-workout, late-night)?

### Takeaway
The clearest, data-backed occasion finding is that froyo visits have shifted toward weekend, planned social outings rather than impulse/kids'-errand stops, with visual/"Instagrammable" presentation and a deliberate "treat yourself" (not diet-food) framing as explicit drivers; evidence for post-workout and late-night as froyo-specific occasions is weak/absent in current trade coverage and relies mostly on generic nutrition-content reasoning rather than category-specific reporting.

### Cited Findings
- Friday and Saturday account for roughly 36% of weekly visits to frozen yogurt chains — higher than ice cream's weekend share — indicating froyo has become "a planned, social outing rather than an impulse stop, more akin to a coffee run than a drive-by treat" — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia)
- 16 Handles' Neil Hershman frames current demand as being about "premium product and better experience where families or friends can hang out," positioning froyo shops as a social/hangout destination rather than a quick grab — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- Trend coverage frames froyo visits explicitly in "treat yourself" terms, with shops "swapping diet culture for indulgence," positioning a froyo visit "whether it's a post-dinner stop or a regular pick-me-up" — [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/usa/news/the-frozen-yogurt-revival-is-officialand-the-fro-yo-is-better-than-you-remember-020426) (opinion/trend journalism, not quantified)
- Visual/social-media appeal is repeatedly cited as a direct driver of visits: "it's important to be different and be Instagrammable," with vibrant toppings, creative flavors, and aesthetically pleasing packaging specifically called out as tactics to attract "tech-savvy generations" — [industry marketing commentary, aggregated]
- Viral, design-forward shops (Myka, Mimi's, Birdie's) are explicitly generating foot traffic through social-media virality and "lines around the block," with some customers timing visits for "froyo for breakfast" specifically to avoid the crowds that build later in the day — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia); [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Go Greek Yogurt's director of strategy and growth reports some customers "coming in multiple times a week," suggesting froyo is functioning as a routine/habitual occasion for a subset of loyal customers rather than only an occasional treat — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/whats-driving-frozen-yogurts-second-act-as-16-handles-and-go-greek-experience-a-renaissance/)
- Extended operating hours (past 8-9 p.m.) are specifically cited by commercial real estate analysis as an important factor in site selection for expanding froyo chains, implying evening/late visits are a meaningful and growing occasion the industry is deliberately building capacity for — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- The high-protein positioning is explicitly linked by Gen Z coverage to fitness/post-workout-adjacent motivations: Gen Z is described as seeking frozen yogurt as a healthier alternative "to bars and alcohol," motivated by "gut health, luxe vibes," and Instagram-worthy presentation — [Fox News / AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-frozen-yogurt-protein-packed-120025000.html)

### Inferences
- The "kids/family" occasion that defined the 2000s-2010s self-serve froyo boom appears to be only partially present in current coverage (Hershman's "families or friends" quote is the only direct reference) — the center of gravity in current trade coverage has shifted toward young-adult (Gen Z/millennial) social and lifestyle occasions, which is a meaningful positioning distinction from the category's earlier heyday.
- Evening/extended-hours site selection (past 8-9pm) suggests late-night is a real, business-validated occasion for the category, even though no trade article explicitly frames a visit as a "late-night" occasion in consumer-motivation language — this is an inference from real-estate/operations decisions rather than a directly cited consumer-behavior claim.

### Gaps
- No quantified, named-source data was found that specifically breaks out "post-workout" as a distinct, trade-press-documented froyo visit occasion; the post-workout connection currently rests on generic nutrition-science reasoning (yogurt/protein/casein benefits) rather than froyo-category-specific consumer research or chain marketing. This should be flagged as inference/opinion, not confirmed occasion data, if used in a briefing.
- No quantified data was found specifically isolating "late-night" as a visit-occasion (e.g., % of transactions after a certain hour) — only the indirect signal of chains extending operating hours past 8-9 p.m.
- No current (2024-2026), froyo-specific "kids/family errand stop" occasion data was found; this classic occasion from the category's earlier boom may be under-covered in current trade press because current coverage skews toward the Gen Z urban revival story — note this as a potential coverage gap rather than evidence the occasion has disappeared.
