# Adjacent/Indirect Dessert & Snack Category Competition with Frozen Yogurt (US)

## How fast are acai bowl chains (Playa Bowls, Vitality Bowls, Nekter Juice Bar) growing in store count/revenue vs. self-serve frozen yogurt chains? Any direct data comparisons?

### Takeaway
Leading acai/juice-bowl chains (Playa Bowls, Nekter Juice Bar, Vitality Bowls) are posting consistent double-digit-to-30%+ multi-year unit growth and rising systemwide sales, while legacy self-serve froyo chains (TCBY, Pinkberry, Orange Leaf) are still shrinking or flat in the US even as the froyo category overall shows renewed (but more modest, and partly foreign-driven) expansion in 2025–2026. No analyst report found puts these two growth rates side-by-side explicitly — the comparison below is assembled from separate company-level data, not a single source.

### Cited Findings
- Playa Bowls ended 2025 with 85 new U.S. shops opened that year and more than 200 signed franchise commitments, nearing/surpassing 400 locations — up from 163 locations three years earlier (net additions of 53, 75, and 81 units over the prior three years, reaching 372 by one count) — [Retail & Restaurant Facility Business](https://retailrestaurantfb.com/playa-bowls-ends-2025-with-85-new-shops-and-200-franchise-commitments/); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/a-retooled-playa-bowls-goes-beyond-400-locations/); [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/issue-archive/2026/january/2026-fast-serious-winners-no-7-playa-bowls/article_a4046cf5-8d8e-4f28-99b5-70b6aeb3aed9.html)
- Playa Bowls generated $332 million in U.S. systemwide sales in 2025, up from $296 million in 2024; systemwide sales rose 89.8% from 2022 to 2024 — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/buying-a-franchise/franchise-profile/how-playa-bowls-does-332-million-in-systemwide-sales)
- Nekter Juice Bar grew from 159 units (2022) to 177 (2023, +11.3%), 193 (2024, +9.0%), and 202 (2025, +4.7%) — a 27% three-year increase, though the rate of new unit additions is decelerating (18 → 16 → 9 net new units/year); plans call for doubling to ~500 locations within 3–5 years — [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/nekter-juice-bar-plans-300-units-five-years/); [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/301-nekter-juice-bar/article_21308181-4232-4865-869f-45e75e1f69f4.html)
- Vitality Bowls grew from 65 units (2024, after a -12.2% contraction year) to 71 units in 2025 (+9.2%), with 8 openings and 1 closure in the most recent reporting year — [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/457-vitality-bowls/article_02e20553-f422-494c-9725-8c9313619910.html)
- By contrast, among legacy self-serve froyo chains in 2024: Menchie's fell to 316 U.S. locations (-10 units); Pinkberry fell to 191 (-62 units); TCBY fell to 123–125 outlets (-16 units), an ~88% decline from its historical peak of roughly 3,000 locations; SweetFrog (250) and Yogurtland (~200–220) were roughly flat — [International Frozen Yogurt Association, "Year in Froyo Review 2024"](https://internationalfrozenyogurt.com/news/the-year-in-froyo-review-2024-edition/); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/retail/iconic-frozen-yogurt-chain-makes-comeback-after-closing-2650-stores-tcby)
- Orange Leaf has contracted roughly 80% from its peak store count to about 67 locations, with continued, named individual closures as recently as December 2025 (New Braunfels, TX, after 15 years) and January 2026 (El Paso, TX, after 14 years) — [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/restaurants/orange-leaf-popular-frozen-yogurt-chain-closes-most-locations)
- At the aggregate level, the froyo category is recovering: the world's top 10 froyo chains grew from roughly 1,956 locations (implied, end of 2023) to 2,267 (end of 2024, +16%/+311 units) to approximately 2,821 (end of 2025, +24% YoY) — but this top-10 list is dominated by non-U.S. brands (Yoajung/South Korea at 564 units, Llaollao/Spain at 317, +44 YoY), so the aggregate growth is not primarily a U.S. phenomenon — [International Frozen Yogurt Association](https://internationalfrozenyogurt.com/news/the-year-in-froyo-review-2024-edition/)
- Within the U.S., smaller/newer froyo concepts are growing briskly off a small base: 16 Handles grew from 29 to 40+ locations with plans for ~25 openings/year through 2027, and Yogurtland targets 22 new openings in 2026 — [ICSC, "Froyo Chains Are Expanding Again"](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- Açaí bowl-focused operators are reported to be growing at roughly twice the rate of traditional juice bars within the broader U.S. juice/smoothie bar sector, which exceeded $780 million in industry revenue in 2025; acai bowls are projected to claim 22% of the $18.7 billion smoothie/juice market by Q3 2025 — [market-research aggregator summary; original report not independently verified — treat as medium confidence]

### Inferences
- Comparing unit-growth trajectories (not sourced side-by-side, but inferred from the company-level data above): the three profiled acai/bowl chains (Playa Bowls, Nekter, Vitality Bowls) are each growing at high-single to low-double-digit percentages annually and have been net openers every year in the 2023–2025 window, whereas the largest legacy U.S. self-serve froyo brands (Menchie's, Pinkberry, TCBY, Orange Leaf) were still net closers in 2024 and have lost 80–90% of peak store counts over the long run. This points to acai/bowl concepts outgrowing froyo on a chain-by-chain basis, even as the froyo *category* overall posted a nominal rebound in 2025–2026 driven by newer, design-forward entrants (see next question's findings on foot traffic) rather than the legacy chains.
- The deceleration in Nekter's and Playa Bowls' unit-growth rates (e.g., Nekter's YoY growth falling from 11.3% to 4.7% over three years) suggests the acai/bowl segment may itself be approaching a maturity inflection, not an unlimited runway — a caveat against over-reading "acai is crushing froyo" as a permanent trend.

### Gaps
- No single analyst, trade press, or market-research source was found that directly compares acai bowl chain growth to self-serve froyo chain growth in one report; all figures above are assembled from separate single-brand sources.
- Revenue/AUV (average unit volume) comparisons between acai bowl chains and froyo chains were not found — only systemwide sales (Playa Bowls) and unit counts, which don't allow a true per-store productivity comparison.
- Vitality Bowls systemwide revenue figures were not found.

## How is the US boba/bubble tea category's growth trajectory, and is there evidence of substitution for frozen yogurt occasions?

### Takeaway
U.S. bubble tea market-size and growth estimates vary widely across research vendors (a sign of low data reliability in this space), but all point to mid-single to high-single-digit percentage annual growth and continued unit expansion by major chains (Gong cha, Kung Fu Tea); no direct evidence (survey, spend-diversion, or real-estate study) was found that specifically documents consumers substituting boba for frozen yogurt occasions — this link remains asserted/inferred in secondary commentary rather than demonstrated with data.

### Cited Findings
- U.S. bubble tea market size estimates for 2025 range widely by vendor: $878.7 million (IMARC Group), $1,075.9 million (Future Market Insights), $750 million (Mark Spark Solutions), $531.69 million (Fortune Business Insights), and $523.07 million (Market Data Forecast) — the near-2x spread between the highest and lowest estimates indicates these are templated market-research products of uncertain methodology, not a single authoritative figure — [IMARC Group](https://www.imarcgroup.com/united-states-bubble-tea-market); [Future Market Insights](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/usa-bubble-tea-market); [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/u-s-bubble-tea-market-107501); [Market Data Forecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-states-bubble-tea-market)
- Projected CAGRs cluster more tightly: 7.39% (IMARC, 2026–2034), 7.22% (Fortune Business Insights, through 2032), 9.6% (Future Market Insights, through 2035) — [same sources as above]
- An earlier-cited (2023-based) estimate put the U.S. bubble tea market growing from $464.29 million (2023) to a projected $750.59 million by 2030 — [secondary blog, tryperdiem.com — original primary source not verified]
- Gong cha operates more than 240 U.S. stores across 23 states as of October 2025, having bought back 170 U.S. stores from a master franchisee to consolidate control and accelerate expansion; the brand pushed back its "500 U.S. locations" target from 2025 to 2028 — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/gong-cha-buys-170-us-master-franchise-stores/814179/); [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/gong-cha-acquires-master-franchise-rights-to-170-u-s-stores-accelerating-national-expansion)
- Kung Fu Tea operates roughly 372 systemwide U.S. locations — [Franchise Times context search result]
- A cited (but not independently verified) CLSA survey finding states that 94% of consumers aged 20–29 bought boba tea in the past three months — [secondary source, tryperdiem.com; primary CLSA report not located — treat as low-to-medium confidence]

### Inferences
- Bubble tea chains and acai/froyo chains compete for overlapping real estate (mall/strip-mall units) and overlapping demographics (teens/young adults, social-media-driven purchase occasions), based on the shared "Gen Z treat occasion" framing used across multiple trade and demographic sources — but this is an inference from parallel category narratives, not a study that measured actual customer overlap or switching.
- Gong cha's buyback of 170 franchised stores and repeated extension of its unit targets suggest the category, like froyo, has faced real operational/profitability friction in scaling quickly in the U.S. — growth is real but not frictionless.

### Gaps
- No survey, consumer panel, or spending-data study was found that directly measures substitution between boba tea and frozen yogurt occasions (e.g., wallet-share shift, same-consumer basket switching, or visit cannibalization).
- No real-estate/leasing data was found specifically comparing boba tea's site-selection overlap with frozen yogurt's trade areas (both reportedly favor malls/strip centers, but no CRE report quantifying competitive overlap was located).
- Given the wide spread in third-party market-size estimates, the "true" U.S. bubble tea market size and growth rate should be treated as uncertain; the CAGR direction (mid-to-high single digits) is more consistently supported than any specific dollar figure.

## Is soft serve (Dairy Queen, convenience-store soft serve) taking share from self-serve froyo specifically, or are they separate markets?

### Takeaway
Available data suggests soft serve/traditional ice cream and self-serve frozen yogurt are behaving as somewhat distinct markets with different trajectories in 2025–2026 — froyo foot traffic has recently outpaced traditional ice cream, and Dairy Queen's U.S. footprint is modestly contracting — which argues against a simple "soft serve is cannibalizing froyo" narrative, though no source directly frames the two as separate or overlapping markets analytically.

### Cited Findings
- Dairy Queen had 4,115 U.S. units in 2025, a net loss of 97 domestic restaurants compared to the prior year, even as the brand pursues a $10 billion global sales target by 2030 (growth is weighted toward international expansion, not U.S. unit growth) — [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/18-dairy-queen/article_517c78b9-34d5-4728-a9af-d4d7887fc850.html); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/dairy-queen-targets-10-billion-by-2030-as-global-growth-continues/)
- Placer.ai foot-traffic data shows frozen yogurt chain visits grew faster year-over-year than "dessert categories" broadly in 15 of 23 months starting January 2024, and in the first half of 2026 froyo visits grew at a positive YoY rate nearly every week (at times exceeding +18%), while traditional ice cream shop visits swung repeatedly negative, falling as low as -8.5% YoY in several weeks during the same period — [Placer.ai, "Frozen Yogurt's Comeback Is More Than Nostalgia"](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia); [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)
- Frozen yogurt visits are concentrated on weekends (Friday+Saturday ≈ 36% of weekly visits), framed by Placer.ai as evidence froyo has become a "planned, social outing" rather than a casual/convenience purchase — a different occasion shape than convenience-store soft serve, which is typically an impulse/convenience purchase — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia)

### Inferences
- The fact that froyo foot traffic is currently outperforming — not underperforming relative to — traditional ice cream/soft serve suggests soft serve is not the primary driver of froyo's historical decline or current competitive pressure; if anything, the 2025–2026 froyo "comeback" is occurring in parallel with, not at the expense of, soft serve chains like Dairy Queen (whose U.S. unit count is shrinking independently of froyo's performance).
- Soft serve (convenience/impulse occasion) and self-serve froyo (increasingly a planned/social occasion per Placer.ai) appear to be differentiating on occasion type, which would support treating them as adjacent but distinct markets rather than direct substitutes — though no source states this conclusion explicitly.

### Gaps
- No data was found specifically isolating convenience-store soft serve (e.g., gas station machines, Carvel, other soft-serve-only brands) performance or growth, separate from Dairy Queen's broader QSR/burger-and-soft-serve format.
- No analyst or trade-press source was found explicitly debating whether soft serve and self-serve froyo are "the same market" or "separate markets" — this framing had to be inferred from indirect foot-traffic and unit-count data, not asserted directly by any source.

## What evidence exists that adjacent categories are directly cannibalizing frozen yogurt's customer base/occasions, vs. separately growing categories?

### Takeaway
The strongest, most concrete data point (Placer.ai foot-traffic data) actually cuts against a cannibalization narrative: frozen yogurt visits have been growing faster than traditional ice cream and faster than "desserts broadly" through 2024–2026, driven by new social-media-forward brands — no survey, spending-diversion, or real-estate-leasing study was found that shows acai bowls, boba, or smoothie bowls are pulling customers or occasions away from froyo specifically; all "cannibalization" framing in available sources is speculative/narrative rather than data-backed.

### Cited Findings
- Frozen yogurt chain visits posted positive YoY growth in nearly every week of the first half of 2026 (at times above +18%), a period coinciding with continued boba and acai bowl chain expansion — i.e., the categories are growing in parallel during the same window rather than one visibly displacing the other in the available traffic data — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia)
- Playa Bowls and Tropical Smoothie Cafe both posted substantial YoY visit growth in Q1–Q3 2024 (Playa Bowls 3.2%–8.7%; Tropical Smoothie Cafe 4.6%–8.6%), attributed by Placer.ai explicitly to "rapidly growing fleets" (i.e., new-unit openings), not stated to be a result of pulling share from froyo or any other specific category — [Placer.ai, "Playa Bowls and Tropical Smoothie Cafe: Berry Big Business"](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/playa-bowls-and-tropical-smoothie-cafe-berry-big-business)
- Playa Bowls' National Acai Day promotion (April 6, 2024, $5 acai bowls) drove a 122.7% single-day visit surge versus the year-to-date daily average — evidence of strong promotional elasticity within the acai category itself, not evidence of cross-category share-shifting — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/playa-bowls-and-tropical-smoothie-cafe-berry-big-business)
- ICSC's real-estate analysis of froyo's 2025–2026 expansion attributes the chains' leasing strategy to targeting "high-income trade areas" and "grocery-anchored centers" in 1,000–1,500 sq ft formats, but does not mention competing for space against acai, boba, or smoothie-bowl concepts — [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/froyo-chains-are-expanding-again)

### Inferences
- Because froyo foot traffic is independently accelerating at the same time acai and boba chains are expanding, the most defensible read of the available evidence is that these are substantially separate, co-existing growth stories (each benefiting from broader "treat/better-for-you" consumer demand, social-media-driven discovery, and better unit economics after a period of overexpansion/contraction) rather than one category winning customers directly from another. This is an inference from the absence of any contrary direct evidence, not a positive finding that cannibalization is absent — it is possible cannibalization exists at the local/trade-area level without being visible in national aggregate foot-traffic trends.
- The lack of any trade-press or analyst source explicitly measuring cross-category substitution (despite extensive search) is itself informative: if cannibalization were a dominant, well-documented industry narrative, it would likely appear in Nation's Restaurant News, QSR Magazine, or Restaurant Business coverage of the froyo "comeback," and it does not.

### Gaps
- No consumer survey data (e.g., "X% of past froyo customers now visit acai/boba shops instead") was found.
- No spending-panel or card-transaction-diversion data (e.g., Earnest Research, Yipit, Facteus-style data) was found for this category set.
- No commercial real estate/leasing report was found that explicitly frames froyo, acai, boba, and smoothie-bowl concepts as competing for the same retail space (beyond the generic observation that all favor similar shopping-center formats).
- No foot-traffic comparison was found that nets out new-unit-driven visit growth (i.e., controls for store count) to isolate same-store/same-market substitution effects between these categories.

## Are frozen yogurt chains responding by adding acai bowls, boba-inspired flavors, or smoothie bowl items to their menus?

### Takeaway
Yes — menu convergence is clearly documented and runs in both directions: established froyo chains (Yogurtland, Menchie's) have added acai bowls to their core menus, while smoothie/bowl chains (Tropical Smoothie Cafe, Planet Smoothie) have added boba-inspired items — indicating category blending rather than one format cleanly replacing another.

### Cited Findings
- Yogurtland launched "Fruit Bowl Fusions and Acai Bowls" as a limited-time summer menu addition in July 2021, offering acai bowls in Strawberry Banana Granola or Peanut Butter styles with a choice of acai or pitaya (dragon fruit) base — [PRNewswire/Yogurtland](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/yogurtland-debuts-refreshing-new-summer-line-up-fruit-bowl-fusions-and-acai-bowls-301324732.html); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/yogurtland-debuts-fruit-bowl-fusions-and-acai-bowls/)
- As of late 2022 reporting, Yogurtland was reported to be developing a new store design and planning to test additional products including acai bowls, shakes, and smoothies — [secondary search-summary citing Restaurant Business Online coverage; the original article could not be directly fetched/verified (403 error) — treat as medium confidence]
- Menchie's Frozen Yogurt currently lists acai bowls as a standing menu category on its own website, offered paired with nonfat frozen yogurt, vegan sorbets, fresh fruit, and granola — [Menchie's official site](https://www.menchies.com/froyo-toppings/acai-bowls/) (primary source, high confidence)
- In the reverse direction, Tropical Smoothie Cafe introduced "Tropiboba Bowls" in 2025 — a limited-time smoothie-bowl item featuring flavored popping boba (Mango Tropiboba with dragon fruit and CocoWhip; Berry Tropiboba with dark chocolate chunks) — showing a smoothie-bowl chain borrowing boba as a menu hook — [PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/its-poppin-tropical-smoothie-cafe-introduces-new-tropiboba-bowls-with-popping-boba-302703148.html); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/tropical-smoothie-cafe-launches-bowls-with-popping-boba/)
- Planet Smoothie's menu includes a "Mango Boba" bowl alongside standard acai and dragon fruit bowls — [Planet Smoothie official site](https://www.planetsmoothie.com/menu/bowls/index.php) (primary source)
- At least one independent/local froyo operator (Yo Factory, Milwaukee) markets acai bowls, frozen coffee and yogurt drinks, and smoothies alongside self-serve frozen yogurt under one roof — illustrative of operator-level category-blending at the single-unit level, though not evidence of a chain-wide strategy — [Yo Factory](https://www.yofactoryyogurt.com/)
- Yo-Chi, described as "Australia's leading self-serve frozen yogurt brand," serves "premium frozen yogurt and organic açaí" together in its format and opened its first U.S. location in Dallas (reported October 3, timeframe consistent with 2025/2026) — another example of a froyo-branded concept built around combining froyo and acai from the outset rather than treating them as separate categories — [Restaurant Magazine](https://restaurantmagazine.com/yo-chi-self-serve-froyo-us-expansion)

### Inferences
- The pattern of bidirectional menu-blending (froyo chains adding acai; smoothie/bowl chains adding boba) suggests operators view these as complementary occasions to capture within a single transaction/visit rather than purely adversarial categories — consistent with the "separate but co-existing growth stories" inference drawn in the cannibalization section above.
- Newer froyo-format entrants (Yo-Chi) are being designed from the start as hybrid froyo-acai concepts, suggesting the industry's own read is that froyo and acai bowls serve an overlapping-but-not-identical occasion best captured together, rather than one being a pure substitute for the other.

### Gaps
- No data was found on the sales mix or incrementality of acai-bowl additions at froyo chains (e.g., what % of Yogurtland or Menchie's revenue now comes from acai bowls vs. traditional froyo) — so it's not possible to say whether this menu diversification is meaningfully offsetting core froyo traffic/revenue pressure or is a minor line extension.
- The Restaurant Business Online article on Yogurtland's 2022 testing plans could not be directly verified (site returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch); findings from it are relayed only via a secondary search-engine summary and should be re-verified by the report writer if used as a key claim.
- No evidence was found of major froyo chains (TCBY, Pinkberry, SweetFrog, Orange Leaf) adding boba specifically (as opposed to acai) to their menus — the boba-adoption evidence found runs through smoothie/bowl chains, not froyo chains.
