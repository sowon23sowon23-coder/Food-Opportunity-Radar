# Frozen Yogurt & Yogurt Category: Market Size, Growth, and Geographic Differences

## What is the current market size and growth rate (CAGR) for frozen yogurt in the US? Is it growing, flat, or shrinking, and since when?

### Takeaway
The US self-serve/retail frozen yogurt category has been in a long structural decline since roughly 2013-2014, after a 2009-2013 boom; recent 2024 point-of-sale data confirms the category is still shrinking in dollar and unit terms, even as market-research forecasters project modest single-digit CAGR growth off a now-smaller base. Published "market size" figures vary wildly by firm (methodology/scope differences), so treat absolute dollar figures as directional, not authoritative.

### Cited Findings
- IBISWorld data (as aggregated by Guidant Financial/industry trackers): US frozen yogurt stores industry revenue was $826.1 million (2020), $802.5 million (2021), and $654 million (2022), with 2022 showing a -3.3% revenue decline — [Guidant Financial / IBISWorld-sourced industry report](https://www.guidantfinancial.com/blog/frozen-yogurt-infographic/)
- The frozen yogurt industry revenue grew at an annualized 22.7% from 2009-2014 (reaching ~$1.8 billion), then contracted at an annualized -5.6% from 2013-2018 — [Restaurant Business Online, "The second frozen yogurt craze continues its long decline"](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/second-frozen-yogurt-craze-continues-its-long-decline) (via search snippet; full article returned 403 on direct fetch)
- TCBY, once the largest US froyo chain with ~3,000 locations at its peak, had shrunk to 406 US locations by 2011 and ~168 domestic franchised locations by 2022, an ~88% decline from peak — [The Daily Meal, "The Rise And Fall Of TCBY"](https://www.thedailymeal.com/1806750/rise-fall-tcby/)
- Red Mango, Cherry Berry, and Orange Leaf are each down at least 60% or more from their store-count peaks — [Guidant Financial, "2019's Top 5 Frozen Yogurt Franchise Brands"](https://www.guidantfinancial.com/blog/2019s-top-5-frozen-yogurt-franchise-brands/)
- The self-serve froyo business model (customers filling their own cups/toppings bars) faced acute health/safety scrutiny during COVID-19 (2020), driving additional permanent closures — [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/second-frozen-yogurt-craze-continues-its-long-decline) (search snippet)
- Circana (NPD-successor) retail scan data cited in trade press: frozen yogurt/tofu dollar sales declined 5% year-over-year to $318 million (unit sales down 3% to 72 million units) in a recent 52-week period in 2024; an earlier-2024 read showed a 5.3% decline to $302 million — [DairyFoods.com trade coverage of Circana data](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/97734-2024-state-of-the-dairy-industry-ice-cream) (full article returned 403 on direct fetch; figures from search-result summary of the article)
- Forward-looking market research (Mordor Intelligence): US frozen yogurt market projected to grow at a 3.69% CAGR from 2025-2030, using 2019-2023 as historical base — [Mordor Intelligence, "United States Frozen Yogurt Market"](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-frozen-yogurt-market)
- Other forecasters show the US frozen yogurt market around $0.92 billion (2025) growing toward ~$970 million by 2032, and North America (US-heavy) holding ~38-42% of global frozen yogurt revenue — [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/industry-reports/frozen-yogurt-market-100799); [search-aggregated figures, multiple firms, Oct 2026 search]
- Growth drivers cited by forecasters (demand-side, not necessarily contradicting the store-level decline): lifestyle/discretionary sweet-indulgence spending, perceived health benefits (digestion, immune function, lower obesity risk vs. ice cream), and non-dairy/plant-based expansion (>25% of US population is lactose intolerant, per Mordor Intelligence) — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-frozen-yogurt-market)
- A structural explanation for the store-channel decline: frozen yogurt "became a victim of its own success" — after Pinkberry popularized it in 2005 as a healthier ice-cream alternative, the product became widely available in grocery store freezers, cannibalizing dedicated froyo shop traffic — [search-result synthesis citing IBISWorld-style analysis, via Gitnux/franchise industry trackers]

### Inferences
- There is a clear bifurcation in the US: the **specialty/self-serve froyo retail format** (TCBY, Red Mango, etc.) has been shrinking for over a decade and continued to shrink through 2024 per Circana scan data, while **forecaster reports** (Mordor, Fortune Business Insights, etc.) show modest CAGR growth — this is likely because those forecasts bundle grocery/retail packaged frozen yogurt (a broader, more stable category) with the declining foodservice/specialty-shop segment, and because low-single-digit forecasts off a shrunken base can still show "growth" in dollar terms due to pricing/inflation even as volumes fall.
- The wide spread in "global/US market size" figures across research firms (e.g., US 2025 figures ranging $0.92B–$0.97B from one set of firms vs. global figures ranging $1.8B–$10.8B+ depending on firm) strongly suggests inconsistent category definitions (fresh self-serve vs. packaged retail vs. "frozen desserts" broadly) — treat any single absolute dollar figure as unreliable without knowing the firm's scope.

### Gaps
- No access to a primary IBISWorld, Mintel, or Statista report with a paywall-free, dated, single authoritative US froyo market size figure for 2024-2026; all figures above are drawn from secondary aggregation/trade press or forecaster marketing pages, several of which could not be fetched in full (403 errors on Restaurant Business Online and Dairy Foods).
- No single most-recent (2025-2026) IBISWorld-style US industry revenue figure was found; the newest hard figure located is 2022 ($654 million, store-format industry).

## How does this compare to the broader frozen desserts / ice cream category's growth?

### Takeaway
Frozen yogurt is underperforming the broader ice cream/frozen dessert category in the US: overall ice cream/sherbet retail sales grew roughly 3% in dollar terms in 2024 even as frozen yogurt/tofu sales fell ~5%, and adjacent "ice milk/frozen dairy dessert" sub-segments grew explosively, pulling share away from froyo.

### Cited Findings
- Circana data (2024, 52 weeks ending Aug. 11): overall ice cream/sherbet category dollar sales rose 3% year-over-year to $8.6 billion, with unit sales up 0.5% to 1.84 billion — [DairyFoods.com coverage](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/97734-2024-state-of-the-dairy-industry-ice-cream) (search-result summary; direct fetch blocked)
- Within that same period, "ice cream" itself (core category) saw sales of $7.4 billion with a slight 1.3% decline, while "ice milk/frozen dairy desserts" saw sales surge 31.6% to $640 million, and "sherbet/sorbet/ices" grew 8.2% to $293 million — [DairyFoods.com coverage](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/97734-2024-state-of-the-dairy-industry-ice-cream)
- By contrast, frozen yogurt/tofu sales fell 5% to $318 million in the same window — [DairyFoods.com coverage](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/97734-2024-state-of-the-dairy-industry-ice-cream)
- US "frozen desserts" market (broad definition, includes ice cream, novelties, froyo, sherbet, etc.) sizing from forecasters: Grand View Research estimates the broader frozen desserts market at ~$21.7 billion (2025) growing to ~$49.1 billion by 2033 (CAGR 10.6%, 2026-2033); other firms (Future Market Insights, Towards FNB) show the US frozen dessert market anywhere from $16.8 billion to $40.5 billion in 2025 depending on scope, growing at CAGRs of roughly 4.5%-5.8% toward 2033-2035 — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-frozen-desserts-market); [search-aggregated, multiple firms]
- US ice cream market alone (IMARC Group) valued at $13.6 billion in 2025 — [IMARC Group, "United States Ice Cream Market"](https://www.imarcgroup.com/united-states-ice-cream-market)

### Inferences
- Frozen yogurt is not just growing slower than the rest of the frozen dessert aisle — it is one of the only sub-segments actually shrinking in dollar terms in the most recent (2024) scanner data, while novelty/frozen-dairy-dessert formats (which may include indulgent or hybrid products) are capturing growth. This points to froyo losing relevance relative to both classic ice cream and newer frozen-dessert hybrids, rather than a category-wide pullback.
- The large discrepancy between "frozen desserts" forecaster CAGRs (4.5%-10.6%) and actual 2024 scanner performance (core ice cream roughly flat-to-down, froyo down 5%) suggests forecaster CAGR figures should be treated skeptically for near-term read-through; they likely reflect long-run nominal/inflation-adjusted modeling rather than the as-reported 2024 consumption trend.

### Gaps
- Could not verify multi-year (2021-2025) trend lines for ice cream vs. froyo from a primary Circana/NPD report; only a single 2024 snapshot (via secondary trade press) was accessible.
- No clean comparison of unit volume (not just dollar sales) growth across all frozen dessert sub-segments beyond what's cited above.

## What is known about the South Korean frozen yogurt / frozen dessert market size and trajectory (froyo boom/bust, 설빙, 요거프레소)?

### Takeaway
Korea's frozen-dessert story diverges sharply from the US: rather than the US's single long decline since 2013, Korea has seen multiple distinct boom/bust cycles — the 빙수(shaved-ice)/설빙 boom (2014-2016) that busted hard, followed by a genuinely new frozen-yogurt-dessert-bar boom led by 요아정(Yoajung) from 2023-2025 that is still accelerating and expanding internationally (including into the US). Note: 설빙's core product (빙수/shaved ice) and 요거프레소's core product (yogurt smoothies/beverages) are not the same category as "frozen yogurt dessert" (요거트 아이스크림) that Yoajung sells — this is an important definitional distinction for any Korea-US comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Sulbing (설빙), Korea's leading 빙수(shaved ice dessert) café chain, saw revenue fall from KRW 20.2 billion (2014) to KRW 12.2 billion (2015) to KRW 9.0 billion (roughly one year later) — a more than 50% revenue collapse within about 3 years — [Hankook Ilbo, "빙수업계 1위 '설빙'이 경고 받은 이유"](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/201606011593448555) (2014-2016 figures — dated historical context)
- Causes cited for Sulbing's decline: seasonality (빙수 is a summer product), store saturation/profitability erosion, a Korean Fair Trade Commission corrective order for failing to disclose nearby franchisee information to 352 prospective franchisees (2014), and trademark disputes in China that stalled its overseas master franchise expansion — [Hankook Ilbo](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/201606011593448555); [search-aggregated coverage]
- Sulbing has since partially recovered: 2024 revenue was KRW 28.1 billion (28억9,968만원 — note: likely intended as 280억9,968만원 i.e. ~KRW 28.1 billion), up 7.6% year-over-year, with a 39.6% operating margin (operating profit KRW 11.1 billion) — [Saramin corporate financial data page for 설빙](https://www.saramin.co.kr/zf_user/company-info/view-inner-finance/csn/bEMrUzIzek1QVDFJajljZ3FUb3M5QT09/company_nm/(%EC%A3%BC)%EC%84%A4%EB%B9%99)
- As of the most recent trade reporting, Sulbing operates ~660 stores in Korea — [Econovill, "홀 매장 늘리는 요아정, '설빙 대항마' 될까"](https://www.econovill.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=673846)
- A 2026 trend piece notes Korean shaved-ice/dessert cafés are shifting toward smaller "cup bingsu" formats as single-person consumption rises and large bingsu prices (mid-teens-thousands to 20,000+ KRW, hotel versions 100,000+ KRW) have pushed cost-conscious consumers away; Sulbing's own cup-bingsu sales reportedly rose 35% with delivery share up 18 percentage points as part of this pivot — [Daum/trend coverage, "빙수마저 1인 1빙?"](https://v.daum.net/v/20260831124304193) (Aug 2026)
- Yogerpresso (요거프레소) — a separate, smaller yogurt-beverage/smoothie franchise chain, not a frozen-yogurt-dessert chain — had average per-franchise annual revenue of KRW 79.33 million in 2024 — [관련 franchise data aggregator](https://www.saramin.co.kr/zf_user/company-info/view-inner-finance/csn/TnZyc2JISXZMR0srcDJVclR5UHQvZz09/company_nm/(%EC%A3%BC)%EC%9A%94%EC%95%84%EC%A0%95) (note: this figure is sourced from search-result synthesis of franchise-cost aggregator sites, not verified against a primary filing)
- **Yoajung (요아정 / "요거트아이스크림의정석")** — Korea's actual frozen-yogurt-dessert-bar boom brand — grew explosively: revenue was KRW 47.1 billion (471억783만원) in 2024, rising to KRW 59.75 billion (597억5,492만원) in 2025, a 26.8% YoY increase — [Innoforest/corporate data via search; Meatlovernews, "탕후루처럼 사라질 줄?... 471억 매출로 반전 드라마 쓴 요아정"](https://www.meatlovernews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=10452)
- Yoajung's offline "Cafe Yoajung" store count rose from 15 (2023) to 374 (2024) — a 359-store increase in one year — while its delivery-only format grew from 166 to 187 stores in the same period; total franchise count now stands at roughly 650+ nationwide — [search-aggregated corporate/franchise data, 2025]
- Yoajung's operating profit reportedly grew roughly 40x in the year following its acquisition by Samhwa (삼화), amid what trade press calls a broader "dessert boom" (디저트 열풍) in Korea — [Nate News, "'요아정'이 뭐길래…삼화 인수 후 1년새 영업익 40배 급증"](https://news.nate.com/view/20250517n08840)
- Yoajung ranked #1 in the dessert category on Korean delivery platforms as of March 2025 — [Nate News, "요아정, 배달 플랫폼 디저트 부문 매출 1위"](https://news.nate.com/view/20250325n28206)
- Yoajung is actively expanding internationally: confirmed 2025 store openings in Australia, Hong Kong, and China, with the brand already present in the US, Singapore, Japan, Taiwan, and Vietnam (8 countries total as of 2025) — [FinanceToday, "요아정, 2025년 호주ㆍ홍콩ㆍ중국 매장 오픈 확정"](https://www.fntoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=343085)
- A trade-press piece flags a risk/debate: whether Yoajung's boom is a durable category shift or a repeat of Korea's earlier "fad dessert" cycles (explicitly compared to the 탕후루/candied-fruit boom-bust and, implicitly, to Sulbing's earlier bingsu bust) — [E-science, "출점 400개 돌파했지만…요아정, 유행 디저트의 전철 밟나"](https://www.e-science.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=126286)
- Broader Korea ice cream market (separate category, includes packaged ice cream generally): reached ~$1.57 billion (2025) projected to reach ~$2.36 billion by 2034 at a 4.63% CAGR (2026-2034) per one forecaster; another forecaster shows $1.60 billion (2025) to $2.53 billion (2035) at 4.70% CAGR — [search-aggregated, multiple market research firms, South Korea ice cream market reports]
- Korea's broader yogurt market (beverage/spoonable yogurt, not frozen) is projected to grow at a 5.9% CAGR from 2025-2033, driven by probiotic-food adoption and high-protein diet preferences — [IMARC Group, "South Korea Yogurt Market"](https://www.imarcgroup.com/south-korea-yogurt-market)

### Inferences
- Korea does not have one continuous "frozen yogurt market" narrative comparable to the US; instead it has a sequence of fad-driven dessert-café boom/bust cycles (빙수/Sulbing in the mid-2010s, 탕후루/candied fruit in 2023, and now 요거트아이스크림/Yoajung in 2023-2025), each centered on a specific franchise format rather than a stable packaged-goods category.
- The Yoajung boom is the most direct Korean analogue to a "frozen yogurt" growth story and is currently in the opposite phase of the cycle from the US market: Korea's version is accelerating (and exporting itself to the US and other markets) at the same time the legacy US froyo retail format continues to shrink. This is a notable and citable US-Korea divergence worth highlighting prominently in the final report.
- Whether Yoajung's boom persists or busts (as trade press itself questions, drawing the parallel to 탕후루) is an open and explicitly flagged risk in Korean trade coverage — this should be presented as a live uncertainty, not a settled growth trend.

### Gaps
- No official Korean government/statistical-agency (KOSIS, 식품의약품안전처) figures on frozen yogurt or dessert-café market size were found in this pass — all figures are franchise-level corporate revenue or trade-press estimates, not category-wide market sizing.
- No independent English-language or international market-research-firm figure specifically sizing "Korea frozen yogurt market" (as distinct from Korea ice cream market or Korea yogurt market) was found; Yoajung's figures are company-level revenue, not an industry-wide "Korean frozen yogurt market" estimate.
- The 요거프레소 (Yogerpresso) figure above is low-confidence — sourced from a franchise-cost aggregator rather than a primary financial filing, and the brand is arguably a different category (yogurt beverages) from frozen yogurt dessert.

## Are there notable global market trends or regional divergences worth flagging (e.g., Asia-Pacific growth vs. North America maturity)?

### Takeaway
Multiple market-research firms converge on the same directional story even where absolute dollar figures diverge: North America (led by the US) is the largest but most mature/slowest-growing frozen yogurt region, while Asia-Pacific — led by China and especially India — is consistently forecast as the fastest-growing region, with double-digit CAGRs well above the US's sub-4% forecast growth.

### Cited Findings
- North America holds roughly 38-42% of global frozen yogurt market revenue as of 2025 per aggregated forecaster figures, reflecting its scale even amid slower growth — [search-aggregated, multiple firms, Oct 2026 search]
- Asia-Pacific is repeatedly cited as the fastest-growing region: one forecaster cites an 11.25% CAGR (through 2035) for Asia-Pacific — the fastest of any region in the global frozen yogurt market; another cites a 10.52% CAGR (2026-2031) for the region — [Mordor Intelligence, "Asia-Pacific Frozen Yogurt Market"](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/asia-pacific-frozen-yogurt-market); [search-aggregated, multiple firms]
- China specifically is forecast to reach ~$2.5 billion by 2030 at an 8.3% CAGR (2022-2030) per one global forecaster — [search-aggregated, ResearchAndMarkets-style report, 2023 vintage]
- India is cited as the fastest-growing major national market within Asia-Pacific, with a 12.31% CAGR forecast through 2031, attributed to organized-retail expansion and rising incomes in tier-2 cities — [search-aggregated, multiple firms, Oct 2026 search]
- Europe is cited by at least one forecaster (OpenPR, citing a market report) as "leading the market through 2030" with a more modest 4.10% CAGR — a claim that appears to conflict with the Asia-Pacific-fastest-growth narrative from other firms and should be treated as a methodology-dependent outlier — [OpenPR, "Frozen Yogurt Market is to grow at 4.10% CAGR, with Europe leading"](https://www.openpr.com/news/3881158/frozen-yogurt-market-is-to-grow-at-4-10-cagr-with-europe-leading) — contradicted by the Asia-Pacific-leadership claims above
- Global frozen yogurt market size estimates for the current period (2025) range enormously by firm — from ~$1.9 billion (Fortune Business Insights) to ~$8.9 billion (Growth Market Reports) to figures as high as $10+ billion from other firms for nearby years — underscoring that "global frozen yogurt market" is not a standardized category across research providers — [search-aggregated, multiple firms, Oct 2026 search]
- Driving factors for APAC growth cited across firms: urbanization, rising disposable incomes, evolving/Westernizing dietary habits, and a shift toward "healthier dessert alternatives" perception of frozen yogurt vs. ice cream — [search-aggregated, Mordor Intelligence and others]

### Inferences
- The clearest, most consistent global pattern across sources (despite absolute-number disagreement) is: **North America = large but mature/slow (~3-4% CAGR, plus active unit-level contraction in the specialty retail format); Asia-Pacific (especially India and China) = smaller base but fastest-growing (~8-12%+ CAGR)**. This divergence mirrors the broader "Western market saturation vs. emerging-market dessert-category growth" pattern seen in other packaged-food categories.
- Korea's Yoajung-driven frozen-yogurt-dessert boom and its active 2025 expansion into other Asian markets (China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Vietnam, Taiwan, Japan) plus into Australia and the US is a concrete, brand-level illustration of the APAC growth narrative that market-research firms describe only in aggregate/forecast terms — it's one of the only sources in this research pass with real, recent (2023-2025) transaction-level growth data rather than a modeled CAGR.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative, cross-checked source (e.g., Statista with a visible primary citation) was found giving one clean global figure; all global sizing claims in this research came from competing paid-report marketing pages with unclear methodological comparability.
- No country-level, dated (2023-2026) hard revenue or CAGR figures were found for APAC markets beyond China and India (e.g., no specific froyo figures for Japan, Southeast Asia broadly, or Korea's own broader category size beyond Yoajung's brand-level revenue).

## What specific factors do analysts cite as driving growth or decline (health trends, inflation/discretionary spending, competition from adjacent categories)?

### Takeaway
Analysts and trade press cite a consistent set of structural factors: self-service froyo's original "healthier than ice cream" positioning has eroded as the format saturated grocery freezers and newer categories (boba, bingsu, candied fruit, dessert bars) captured discretionary treat spending; inflation and value-consciousness are squeezing larger-format desserts in favor of smaller/cheaper formats (e.g., Korea's "cup bingsu" shift); and in the US, pandemic-era hygiene concerns specifically damaged the self-serve topping-bar model that defined the category.

### Cited Findings
- Frozen yogurt specialty shops were undercut by their own packaged-retail success: once Pinkberry (founded 2005) popularized frozen yogurt as a healthier ice cream alternative, the product spread into grocery store freezers, reducing the need for dedicated specialty shop visits — [search-aggregated synthesis of industry-report commentary, e.g. Gitnux/franchise trackers]
- The COVID-19 pandemic (2020) intensified hygiene concerns specific to the self-serve/communal-topping-bar froyo model, driving additional permanent store closures beyond the pre-existing secular decline — [Restaurant Business Online, search-result summary](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/second-frozen-yogurt-craze-continues-its-long-decline)
- Forecasters cite lactose intolerance (>25% of the US population, per Mordor Intelligence) as a driver of non-dairy/plant-based frozen yogurt product line growth, a sub-segment seen as a growth pocket within an otherwise shrinking category — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-frozen-yogurt-market)
- General (not froyo-specific) 2024 consumer-trend data: 30% of US consumers sought low-sugar food/beverage options in 2024, up 3 points from the prior year, and 44% of consumers said they seek "natural" sweeteners vs. 21% preferring artificial ones — relevant context for why a sugar-heavy treat category faces headwinds, though no source directly ties this stat to frozen yogurt specifically — [NIQ/Nielsen, "Wellness trends influencing consumers in 2024"](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/analysis/2024/wellness-trends-influencing-consumers-in-2024/)
- In Korea, rising prices for dessert-café formats (large bingsu now commonly KRW 15,000-20,000+, hotel versions 100,000+ KRW) are cited as pushing cost-conscious, increasingly single-person households toward smaller, cheaper "cup" formats — a value/inflation-driven format shift rather than a category abandonment — [Daum trend piece, Aug 2026](https://v.daum.net/v/20260831124304193)
- Korean trade press explicitly frames the current Yoajung dessert boom within a pattern of fast-moving "fad dessert" cycles (comparing it to the 2023 탕후루/candied-fruit boom, which subsequently busted), suggesting analysts view rapid franchise-count growth with some skepticism about durability — [E-science](https://www.e-science.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=126286)
- US forecaster commentary attributes continued (modeled) category growth to "changes in lifestyle, increased expenditure on food and beverages, and increasing preference for sweet indulgence," alongside perceived digestive/immune-health benefits — i.e., the same "healthier indulgence" positioning that originally drove the 2009-2013 US boom is still cited as a growth driver in current forecasts, even though real 2024 scanner data shows continued category contraction — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-frozen-yogurt-market)

### Inferences
- There is a disconnect between the demand-side narrative used by market-research firms to justify forecast growth (health positioning, indulgence spending, non-dairy expansion) and what 2024 US retail scanner data actually shows (continued dollar and unit decline for frozen yogurt specifically, while other frozen dessert sub-segments grow). This suggests the "healthier than ice cream" health-halo positioning that once drove the category's two historical US booms (TCBY-era and Pinkberry-era) has largely stopped converting into category growth, likely because it has been out-competed by other "healthier/better-for-you" dessert formats (e.g., protein ice cream, frozen dairy desserts, or in Korea's case, the Yoajung format itself, which also markets a yogurt/health angle but in a novel delivery format).
- Inflation/value-consciousness appears to affect format and price-point (smaller cups, delivery) more than it kills demand outright — both the Korean bingsu-to-cup-bingsu shift and general sugar-reduction consumer trends point to consumers trading down in format/price rather than abandoning the dessert occasion.

### Gaps
- No analyst source was found directly and explicitly attributing the 2024 US frozen yogurt sales decline to a named competing category (e.g., explicitly naming boba/bubble tea, protein ice cream brands, or Dole Whip-style novelties as the specific substitute); this causal link is inferred from general trend data, not a direct citation, and should be flagged as such in the final report.
- No hard data was found on US inflation's specific impact on froyo discretionary spending (e.g., a cited price-elasticity or consumer-spending-pullback statistic specific to frozen yogurt); the inflation/value-consciousness driver is better evidenced for Korea (bingsu format) than for the US in this research pass.
